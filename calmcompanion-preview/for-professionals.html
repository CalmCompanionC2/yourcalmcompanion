For professionals

A tool for the toolbox.

Norman brings deep pressure, gentle haptic rhythm and co-regulation through touch into one object a person can hold. He is designed to support self-regulation. He is not a medical device and not a substitute for clinical care.

Where he fits

One companion, many settings.

Therapy and OT practices

Four modes to match the session. Responsive Hug for engagement, Box Breathing for pacing, Heartbeat and Deep Calm for winding down.

Schools and classrooms

A quiet, screen-free regulation tool for a calm corner or a student's desk. No app, no notifications, nothing to set up.

Pediatric and dental clinics

Something soft to hold in the waiting room or the chair. Easy to clean and nothing wireless in the room.

Waiting rooms of every kind

Clinics, offices and hospitals. A steady rhythm for anyone waiting on something that matters.

What Norman adds

Familiar techniques, in one hand.

Each mode is built on an approach practitioners already know. Norman simply makes it something a person can hold and follow on their own.

Deep pressureA weighted body with glass bead fill, plus a squeeze that is answered in kind.
Paced breathingBox Breathing keeps a four-count so the person can follow without watching a screen.
Tension and releaseDeep Calm rises quickly, then eases down for much longer.
Slowing rhythmHeartbeat gradually slows across five minutes.
Co-regulationA warm, steady presence that offers a calm signal to follow.

Beyond a script

What Norman adds between sessions.

Verbal coaching works. Norman gives a client something to hold while they practice it.

Consistency

The same rhythm, the same count and the same release every time, with no variation in pacing or tone.

A tactile anchor

Something physical to hold on to, which can help a client stay with the practice when attention drifts.

Take-home use

A client can use it on their own between sessions, with no app, no screen and nothing to set up.
