For professionals

A tool for the toolbox.

A calm, screen-free tool for therapy rooms, classrooms and waiting rooms. Norman brings weight, gentle haptic rhythm and a steady presence together in one object a person can hold. He is designed to support self-regulation and is not a medical device.

Tell us about your setting

Concept render

Where he fits

One companion, many settings.

Therapy and OT practices

Four modes to match the session. Responsive Hug for engagement, Box Breathing for pacing, Heartbeat and Deep Calm for winding down.

Schools and classrooms

Something a student can pick up on their own, without a screen or a distraction. Norman fits a calm corner, a desk or a counselor's office, with no app and nothing to set up.

Pediatric and dental clinics

For long dental visits, Norman offers a comforting weight, gentle vibration and a friendly face. In pediatric offices, he is an easy icebreaker and a companion who stays by their side.

Waiting rooms of every kind

Clinics, doctors' offices, hospitals and veterinary clinics. A steady rhythm for anyone waiting on something that matters, including pet owners.

What Norman adds

Familiar techniques, in one companion.

Each mode is built on an approach practitioners already know. Norman simply makes it something a person can hold and follow on their own.

Deep pressureA comforting weight, soft fur and a hug that answers back.
Paced breathingBox Breathing keeps the count by touch: four in, four held, four out, four held.
Tension and releaseDeep Calm follows tension and release with a quick firm build, then a long gentle ease.
Slowing rhythmHeartbeat mode eases into a slower rhythm over five minutes.
Co-regulationA warm, steady presence that offers a calm signal to follow.

Beyond a script

What Norman adds between sessions.

Between sessions, Norman gives a client something to hold that supports regulation at home, at school or on the go.

Consistency

The same rhythm, the same count and the same release every time, with no variation in pacing or tone.

A tactile anchor

Something physical to hold on to, which can help a client stay with the practice when attention drifts.

Take-home use

A client can use it on their own between sessions, with no app, no screen and nothing to set up.
