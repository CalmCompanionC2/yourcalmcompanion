Meet Norman

Soft to hold.
Steady to follow.

Norman is a big, soft, weighted Highland cow, and holding him is only the start. He has four modes: he answers your hug, keeps a heartbeat that slows, rolls through a long wave of tension and release, or paces your breath. Each is built on the science of how the body and mind settle.

Four modes

Pick one. Watch him move.

Norman has five small motors inside. Three are precise, giving the crisp beats and taps, like a heartbeat or the count of a breath. Two are soft and ambient, adding a low hum that swells and fades. Each mode uses them in its own combination.

What's inside

Soft on the outside. Considered within.

Weight

Deep pressure comfort

He is a chunky one, clocking in at 5 lbs, and the heft is the point. His settled, even weight feels calming and grounding in your lap or against your chest, and he stays put.

Touch

Answers when you hold him

A pressure sensor under the heart patch on his upper back reads how firmly you press in Responsive Hug, and runs Box Breathing for as long as you hold.

Simple

Nothing to manage

No app, no screen and no wireless. WiFi and Bluetooth are off in firmware. One button and four colors, one for each mode, are all there is to learn.

Everyday life

Easy to live with.

Battery

About 20 days

Per charge, at around 30 minutes of use a day.*

Run time

Sessions that fit your day

Heartbeat runs 5 minutes and Deep Calm runs 10. Responsive Hug and Box Breathing run for as long as you hold him.

Controls

One button

No app and nothing to set up. Everything happens on the button on his side.

Portable

Easy to take along

About 5 lb and about the size of a throw pillow.

Charging

Charge with USB-C

Use a standard USB-C cable and power adapter. The port is on his back, and a full charge takes roughly 4 to 7 hours.*

Status light

A light for the battery

A red pulse means he is charging. A gold pulse means the battery is low, and it only shows while he is asleep, so it never interrupts a session.

Without vibration

His weight can be enough on its own

You do not have to turn him on every time. Some days, simply holding him close, with his 5 lb of weight, is enough.

*Early estimates, not yet independently verified.
