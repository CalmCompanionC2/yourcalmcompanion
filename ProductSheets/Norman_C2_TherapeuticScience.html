Pillar 01 · Deep pressure stimulation
⚖️
Peer-Reviewed Research · Occupational Therapy
Deep pressure activates the parasympathetic nervous system
Deep Pressure Stimulation (DPS) — the kind produced by weighted objects held against the body — is a well-established therapeutic intervention. When a person holds or squeezes a weighted object, pressure activates proprioceptors that signal the brain to shift from sympathetic ("fight or flight") to parasympathetic ("rest and digest") mode. This triggers a measurable neurochemical cascade: serotonin and dopamine production increases while cortisol — the primary stress hormone — decreases. A 2025 systematic review confirmed strong evidence for deep pressure tactile input positively impacting functional outcomes including sleep, attention, motor skills, and anxiety across populations. Occupational therapists reference deep touch pressure as producing therapeutic benefits including lowered anxiety and increased calm, citing its effectiveness even in high-stress environments such as medical settings. Norman operationalizes this directly: a force-sensitive resistor on Norman's back means Responsive Hug mode responds in real time to how hard the user squeezes, actively engaging this same proprioceptor pathway rather than relying on static weight alone.

Pillar 02 · Therapeutic haptic vibration
🌊
Multiple Independent Research Programs
Vibration therapy measurably improves nervous system regulation and reduces stress
Haptic vibration's therapeutic effects are validated across multiple independent research programs. Vibroacoustic therapy (Sandler et al.) shows vibration through physical contact produces measurable relaxation via skin conductance changes. A 2025 PMC study found gradually decreasing tempo vibration reduces acute stress and lowers cortisol. A wearable study (Azevedo et al., Scientific Reports, 2017) found steady vibration paced slower than resting heart rate significantly reduced anxiety — passive entrainment, with no conscious effort required. Norman's Deep Calm mode also draws on Progressive Muscle Relaxation: since Jacobson's 1930s work, decades of research confirm brief tension followed by a markedly longer release produces greater relaxation than release alone — the same asymmetric shape Norman's haptic sequence follows. Apollo Neuro's 17+ clinical trials† further validate the mechanism, including a double-blind trial showing measurable HRV improvement. Norman implements this through a five-motor, dual-motor-type system — precision LRA motors for directional patterns, ERM motors for continuous presence.‡

NIH Research · 2024 · Most Directly Applicable to Norman
Weight + vibration combined is more effective than either alone
A 2024 study in the NIH research database examined the combination of weighted pressure with vibrotactile stimulation — precisely the combination Norman delivers. The study measured physiological anxiety markers including pulse rate and skin conductance and found statistically significant reductions in both. The conclusion: vibrotactile-weighted interventions effectively reduce anxiety as indicated by both physiological measures and subjective comfort ratings, and the findings support their potential as a formal therapeutic tool. This is structurally what Norman delivers: a glass-bead-weighted plush body providing the deep pressure component, combined with Norman's dual-motor-type vibrotactile system providing the vibration component — the same two-mechanism combination shown here to outperform either alone. Simple weighted-and-vibrating comfort objects exist; what's not yet on the market is this combination delivered through a multi-motor, mode-driven haptic system rather than a single fixed-speed motor.

Pillar 03 · Co-regulation through touch
🫶
Neuroscience · Multiple Sources · 2022–2025
Touch is a primary nervous system regulation tool — with measurable neurochemical effects
Touch is now established in peer-reviewed neuroscience as a primary pathway for nervous system regulation. Research published in Frontiers in Psychology (2025) found that even a brief 20-second compassionate self-touch can effectively reduce stress — and that those who use touch as an emotion regulation tool consistently benefit more from affective touch overall. A 2024 systematic review confirmed that co-regulation through touch is strongly linked to improved self-regulation outcomes, with nurturing touch triggering oxytocin increase and cortisol decrease as measurable neuroendocrine markers. Research from eLife Sciences (2023) demonstrated that social touch activates central nervous system oxytocin mechanisms that modulate endocrine stress outcomes. Frontiers in Pediatrics (2023) found that therapeutic affective touch produces beneficial changes in inflammation, immunity, and stress at a physiological level. Norman's haptic vibration is designed to mimic this signal — a calm, steady external stimulus intended to communicate safety to the nervous system through the same tactile pathways.

Why Norman is different from everything else.
vs. Weighted blankets
Portable + interactive

Therapeutic tools work best when available in the moment of need. Norman is always there — at home, at work, at night. The nervous system doesn't keep office hours.
