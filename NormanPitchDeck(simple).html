Section 02 · Continued
What Norman
actually does
Four distinct therapeutic techniques, each with its own motor choreography — not four settings on the same vibration.
01 · Responsive Hug
A continuous, real-time biofeedback loop — squeeze harder, feel more; release, and it falls silent. No fixed duration, no script. It responds to you.
02 · Heartbeat
A lub-dub pulse that slows itself — from 40 down to 33 BPM over five minutes, gently pacing the body back toward calm.
03 · Deep Calm
Built on Progressive Muscle Relaxation — a slow rise-hold-fall rhythm that fades to near-silence by the end of a ten-minute session.
04 · Box Breathing
A tactile metronome guides a four-phase breath — inhale, hold, exhale, hold — advancing only while you stay engaged.
The engineering underneath
Three independently-addressed motors, capable of true simultaneous activation on a single shared circuit — layered with two continuous ambient motors, and read in real time by a pressure sensor most comfort objects don't have at all.

Appendix
What didn't
make it
The finished product is the easy part to show. What's harder to see is everything that was built, tested, and deliberately thrown away to get there.
Deep Calm Mode — five rejected engines
A fixed 0.1Hz rate. A moving pressure band. Tide-plus-ripple layering. ERM-only continuous presence. Sparse, irregular touches. All five were fully built and tested before a sixth — an asymmetric rise-hold-fall envelope modeled on Progressive Muscle Relaxation — was confirmed as the strongest candidate and became what ships today.
Box Breathing's click timing
An early scheduler placed the fourth tactile click exactly on the phase boundary, where it lost a timing race and silently never fired. Found through real-hardware testing, not a spec review — timing was rebuilt to stay clear of that boundary entirely.
Box Breathing's pause behavior
Originally always preserved position on any pause. Real testing showed that made it hard to tell where a resumed session was rejoining the cycle — reworked into a threshold: brief pauses resume, longer ones restart fresh at the next inhale.
